# Quarter 1 rules and balancing

The immutable rule version is `q1-1.0-provisional`. Curriculum completion and all three understanding answers precede the five-coin reward. The reward is recorded once at business creation, as a reward—not sales revenue.

- Bento prices: 4–8. Base production cost: 3 per successful sale only.
- Customer deck: `[4,4,5,5,6,6,6,7,7,8,8,10]`, shuffled deterministically on the server and recycled after exhaustion.
- Big Sale: keep price or discount by one for that opportunity, before revealing three customer budgets. Bonus advertising visitors are additive.
- Bank: transfer 0–5 affordable coins to protected savings. No revenue/profit/expense change.
- Advertising: spend 0/2/4 to add 1/2/3 visitors to the next sales opportunity. These are opportunities, not guaranteed buyers.
- Returns: the most recent unreturned sale is eligible. Refund its sale price, or spend 3 on a replacement. A server die of 4–6 succeeds; 1–3 also refunds the original sale. A sale is never returned twice.
- Price revision: one change of at most one coin, constrained to 4–8, after Sales Day, Market Change, or Sparko’s Bench.
- Town Hall: no Quarter 1 tax. Festival contribution: voluntary 0–2 affordable coins, recorded as an expense.
- Wallet shortfalls from mandatory costs/refunds create a recovery advance liability. Advances never count as revenue. Savings are not confiscated. The journal supports affordable repayment.
- The first lap stops at Start even if the die would pass it. No second quarter is silently entered. A written reflection of 12–500 characters and a confidence rating unlock exploration.

Profit is sales revenue minus production costs, business expenses, and refunds. Wallet balance additionally reflects gifts, rewards, savings transfers, and loans. These must never be conflated.

All eight Market Change and twenty Omikuji names from the brief are included. The brief did not provide all monetary values. `content.ts` centralizes the explicit provisional amounts and opportunity effects. They need product-owner balancing approval.

Multiplayer supports 2–4 private-code participants. Dice and card processing run through the same server transition logic; turns advance after a pending decision resolves. Markets are shared as next-sales-opportunity effects. This is a documented approximation of the brief’s exact shared-one-round expiry, which is not yet implemented. Personal fortunes and finances remain private.

Current teaching examples are denominated only in fictional LEAD Coins and do not offer real-world investment advice.
